# Community qualification

Use a curated Community Registry as a high-confidence seed, not a closed allowlist.
Record each community's languages, catchment, Evidence Roles, governance signals,
access methods, qualification date, and review status.

Quarantine newly discovered communities as Community Candidates until assessed.

## Qualification assessment

| Dimension | Questions |
|---|---|
| Governance | Are rules enforced and abuse corrected in inspectable ways? |
| Identity continuity | Can readers distinguish sustained participants or accountable contributions? |
| Discussion provenance | Are dates, thread boundaries, authorship context, and stable links visible enough to audit? |
| Topic fit | Does the community contain relevant experience or scrutiny, rather than keyword coincidence? |
| Catchment | Whose local, diaspora, professional, or global perspective does it reflect? |
| Search accessibility | Can candidate URLs be lawfully discovered and retrieved under current authorization? |
| Manipulation risk | What incentives, brigading, sponsorship, or astroturfing risks apply? |

Do not require voting, real names, or public moderator logs. Locally appropriate
accountability can come from editorial curation, invitation, professional identity,
long-lived pseudonyms, moderator intervention, visible corrections, or other
mechanisms that make contributions inspectable and costly to abuse.

Qualification makes a community eligible for a portfolio; it does not make every
thread trustworthy. Assess Evidence Quality per discussion.

Domain-scoped general web search is the default discovery layer. APIs, feeds, and
native public search may improve recall but are not qualification requirements.
Keep discovery separate from credential-mediated direct retrieval.
